Research

01

Knowledge in LMs

Language models are increasingly used as substitutes for search, yet they remain static snapshots of their training data and are prone to hallucination. How can we make them reliably incorporate new information while preserving what they already know?

02

Pretraining Science

How does pretraining shape downstream behaviors? How should we structure pretraining, midtraining, etc. to obtain a model that is a) maximally amenable to post-training, and b) robust to forgetting?

03

Agentic Capabilities

How can we build models that can succeed at problems requiring increasingly long trajectories?

About

Currently a rising third-year PhD student at Cornell University, I work under the guidance of Prof. Tanya Goyal on problems at the core of natural language processing. My research revolves around knowledge in language models; how they store it, how we can edit it, and how we can calibrate them to be more reliable and factual.

This semester (Fall 2026), I am also working as an intern at Datology, with Pratyush Maini .

I'm also deeply motivated by empirically grounded research that helps us understand language models better; scaling laws, physics of LMs, and the broader science of deep learning.

I obtained my undergraduate degree in Mathematics at UT Austin, where I was first introduced to NLP. I was fortunate to work with Prof. Eunsol Choi, Prof. Greg Durrett, and Prof. Richard Tsai.
